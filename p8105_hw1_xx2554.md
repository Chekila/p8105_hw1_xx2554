The mean works for the numeric and logical variables, but not for the
character or factor variables. The mean of `random_sample` is 0.4128956.
For the logical variable, R treats `TRUE` as 1 and `FALSE` as 0, so its
mean is 0.6, which means that 60% of the values are `TRUE`. The
character and factor variables do not have directly meaningful numeric
means.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, positive))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, character_vec))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, factor_vec))
```

When the logical variable is converted to numeric, `FALSE` becomes 0 and
`TRUE` becomes 1. Therefore, the mean of a logical variable can be
calculated, and it represents the proportion of `TRUE` values.

The character variable cannot be converted into meaningful numeric
values, so the conversion produces `NA`. This also explains why the mean
cannot be calculated for the character variable.

When the factor variable is converted to numeric, R returns the numeric
codes of its factor levels. In this case, `large`, `medium`, and `small`
are coded as 1, 2, and 3. These numbers are only internal codes for the
categories and do not have meaningful numerical values, so taking the
mean of a factor variable is not meaningful.
